# Product Rules

Implemented rules for the coordinated Symfony, browser and Android-client logic upgrade. These are behavior and limits, not promises of safe access, fish availability or a production release. See [README](README.md) for local operations and [Authentication](AUTHENTICATION.md) for account/API details.

## Ownership And Privacy

- Private operations require a verified bearer session. Legacy accounts remain stored but can only be activated after operator-verified ownership and an invitation. Knowing a username, being the first claimant or presenting the old identity header grants nothing. Administrators manage accounts; that role does not grant another owner's private diary/library access through these APIs.
- Both clients create trips privately. Live trips are never publicly readable, including legacy live rows marked public. Completing a live trip leaves it private. Only completed trips can be published; the clients provide a separate review of the saved version. Existing completed/public rows retain their publication flag with conservative migrated sharing defaults.
- Publication defaults to `publicLocationPrecision: approximate`, `shareNotes: false` and no `sharedMediaIds`. Approximate coordinates are rounded to two decimal places, with generic public location labels and no owner username or original spot identifier. Exact coordinates require an explicit choice. Notes sharing includes trip/fish notes and bait text; only selected, already-uploaded media belonging to the trip is public. New photos are not automatically selected.
- Saving changes to an already-public trip updates its public version under the existing sharing choices. Make it private before adding sensitive details. Owners can change publication choices or delete their trips/media. Suspension revokes sessions/invitations and hides the owner's published trips and media; it is not data erasure. Reactivation can expose still-public completed trips again, without restoring revoked credentials.
- API JSON and trip-media responses use `private, no-store`, with visibility checked again on media requests. This limits future caching; it cannot retract downloads, screenshots, third-party copies or caches made by older releases. Approximation and stripped identity fields are **not anonymity**: timestamps, catches, recognizable photos and shared text can reveal a person or exact location. Image re-encoding does not remove visible location clues.

## Search And Evidence

- Current clients send structured `technique` and `location`, optional `targetSpecies`/`fishingAt`, and explicit `mode`, `radiusKm` (1-60), `resultLimit` (1-48) and `saveHistory`. GPS/pin searches carry `coordinates`/`locationLabel`, with `gpsAccuracyM` when available; point mode requires coordinates. Supported techniques are surfcasting, spinning, shore jigging, eging, bottom fishing, rock fishing and boat fishing. Query-only parsing remains for shipped legacy searches, not for authentication.
- `typicalSpecies` and target-species compatibility describe a general technique profile. They do not establish presence, seasonal abundance, activity, absence of an unlisted species or catch probability. No personal diary data trains or improves the ranking.
- Without `fishingAt`, the API requests current provider conditions. A UTC ISO timestamp requests **actual hourly weather/marine forecast data** within the next seven days, selecting an available hour within 30 minutes. Historical and more distant searches are rejected. An unavailable forecast hour/provider produces an error, never current weather relabeled as a forecast.
- Preserve `validAt` (model time), `fetchedAt` (retrieval time), `sourceCoordinates`, `requestedCoordinates` and `temporalMode`; `generatedAt` is not the observation time. Nearby results have `conditionsScope: regional`: shared conditions from the search center, not independently checked conditions at each spot. Point analysis requests conditions at its analyzed location, which may be a neighboring modeled water sample rather than the requested standing point. Provider grid coordinates can differ from both.
- Nearby assessment considers at most 48 OSM candidates and eight directions per candidate, independently of the requested display count. Known mapped `no`/`private` fishing/access restrictions are excluded. Unknown access remains unknown, not permission. OSM labels, nearby modeled water and a point pin do not verify legal access, a walkable route, a safe standing place or an unobstructed cast. Point analysis does not perform a comprehensive restriction lookup.
- Scores mean `heuristic-fit`. `recommendationStatus` (`eligible`, `caution`, `unverified`, `unsuitable`), reasons and `conditionsStatus` distinguish adverse signals from unknown data. Conservative thresholds are **not a safety assessment or catch prediction**; even `no-adverse-signal` does not mean safe. Missing/stale critical data cannot count as favorable evidence.
- EMODnet DTM values are native-cell means at roughly **115 m resolution**, not sonar measurements. They do not measure seabed material or snag risk. There is no fine casting target: optional `approximateZone` is a low-confidence direction/distance/depth range with `actionable: false`, not target coordinates. It is withheld when evidence, GPS accuracy, technique range or conditions do not support even that coarse zone. No navigation or relocation route is provided.

## Diary And Outcomes

- One live trip per account can be active; resume or finish it before starting another. A new historical entry is created completed and does not replace the active trip. Recording mode cannot later be switched, and completed trips cannot be reopened through the API.
- `tripDate` is actual fishing start, `endedAt` is actual end, and `completedAt` is the application's completion/entry action time. They are not interchangeable. New historical entries require an actual end and explicit outcome; current clients also require an actual end when finishing live trips. Dates need valid timezone-aware values and cannot be future fishing records. Legacy completed entries keep an unknown `endedAt` unless corrected by the owner; editing notes/catches does not require inventing it.
- `zero` means explicitly reported no catch; `not-recorded` means no known result. Neither has fish records. `recorded` requires fish records. An empty log is not automatically a zero, and empty live completion requires an explicit outcome. Removing the final fish without an explicit replacement outcome returns the result to unrecorded, not zero.
- Fish records retain stable IDs during corrections so existing photos remain attached. Measured groups require an explicit weight/length basis; unchanged legacy groups without one remain unknown, not silently multiplied or reinterpreted. Removing a fish removes its attached media when saved.
- Fishing minutes and angler count are optional: blank is unknown, never zero or an assumed single angler. Supplied values must be positive integers; anglers are limited to 100. Minutes require an actual end and cannot exceed elapsed duration beyond one minute of rounding tolerance. Clients retain unfinished effort in the local draft until an actual end can be supplied.
- `/api/insights` summarizes only the owner's completed records. Catch per angler-hour (CPUE) uses fish and angler-hours from the **same known-outcome, valid-effort cohort**, including explicit zero-catch trips. Unknown outcomes/effort and active trips do not enter that rate. Total known fish can include trips excluded from CPUE; no usable effort means a null rate, not zero. These are descriptive, self-reported observations, not predictive learning or evidence of causation.
- Search snapshots are not observed trip conditions. Server validation requires time alignment with `conditionsRecordedAt` and the fishing interval (one-hour tolerance); unmatched conditions become unknown while the original evidence is retained privately in `sourceSnapshot`, not exposed in public projections. Current clients do not copy today's conditions into new historical entries. Retained legacy snapshots do not prove past weather, and reopening a saved scan does not refresh its conditions.

## Local Drafts

- **Browser:** drafts and continuation state use local storage scoped to the browser origin, verified account ID and trip/new destination. They survive closing the browser and session expiry/401, but are locked until that same account is verified again. Explicit logout or account switching removes the prior account's drafts/continuation. A new guest selection takes precedence over an old continuation. Tokens use session storage by default; the explicit remember choice uses local storage.
- **Android/Capacitor:** drafts use local storage scoped to API URL, verified account ID and trip/new destination. Expiry/401 preserves them for same-account, same-server reauthentication while private/map state is cleared. Another account cannot open that scope. Explicit logout clears all session/draft data for the selected API URL; changing server also clears the old and selected new URL's stored session/draft scopes. Tokens use session storage, not persistent local storage; a remembered username is only a login hint.
- Neither client exposes account drafts to guests. Saving clears the saved draft fields, not unrelated pending edits/deferred effort; discard and logout are deliberate local-data removal actions. Successful uploads are already server data and are not undone by discarding a draft. Storage can fail or be cleared by the browser/OS: drafts are not an encrypted vault, server backup, offline synchronization queue or guarantee that unuploaded image selections survive a restart. Logout attempts server revocation; if the network fails, that session may remain valid until expiry or later revocation.

## Libraries And Retention

- Search history is opt-in (`saveHistory: true`) for authenticated owners and defaults off. Guests create no new scan records; requesting saved history without authentication is rejected. Owners can view/delete one scan or all their scans, delete saved places, and delete their trips/media. These are separate collections: deleting history does not delete trips or bookmarks.
- Existing stored scans, including legacy anonymous scans, are preserved. Owner-scoped endpoints do not expose or auto-assign anonymous rows, and there is **no automatic purge** of that legacy data. New saved history is retained until owner deletion; broader retention/backup cleanup needs an explicit operator policy.
- The public place catalog exposes trusted `provenance: osm` entries only. Migration marks old catalog rows `legacy`, not public; a newly trusted OSM search can refresh an OSM entry. Private trip locations, bookmarks, point/fallback labels and legacy catalog rows do not become public simply because they are stored. Deleting private history does not delete the independent OSM catalog.
- Saved places are private and limited to 500 per owner. Re-saving the same account/coordinates (database precision: seven decimals)/technique is idempotent: it returns the existing ID and preserves its name and notes. Saving again is not an implicit notes-edit operation.

## External Limits

Geocoding, OSM coverage, bathymetry, weather/marine models, map imagery and GPS depend on external services or device permissions. Outages, rate limits, missing cells and stale/incomplete observations can yield errors, fewer results or explicitly unknown conditions. Missing spots/depths are not fabricated to fill a result count. Forecast availability is limited by the provider, even inside the accepted seven-day window.

Opting out of saved history is not a promise of zero location processing: providers receive the relevant query/coordinates, and shared upstream geodata/depth caches and operational logs can still exist. History deletion does not purge those caches, provider retention or backups. Check current official weather/marine warnings, local fishing rules, signage, access and equipment on site; the app cannot replace them.
